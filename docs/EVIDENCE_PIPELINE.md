# Evidence pipeline

The evidence pipeline lives in `src/evidence/evidence-pipeline.js`. It keeps provider artifacts untrusted and produces a versioned `workrelay.evidence-manifest.v1` record through the canonicalization and SHA-256 commitment layer.

`captureSource()` is the source-fetching boundary. It allows HTTPS only on the default port, rejects credentials and fragments, and bounds redirects (each hop is revalidated), response bytes and total time (`timeoutMs`, default 10 s, maximum 30 s, one deadline covering DNS, every hop and the body).

**Address policy.** Each hop resolves the hostname once and every returned address must be public. IPv4 is checked against a block list (this-network, private, shared CGNAT, loopback, link-local, protocol and documentation ranges, benchmarking, multicast and reserved). IPv6 is an allow list: only global unicast `2000::/3` is accepted, minus special-purpose ranges inside it (`2001::/23`, `2001:db8::/32`, `2002::/16`, `3fff::/20`). Loopback, unspecified, link-local `fe80::/10` (including `fe90::` to `febf::`), unique-local `fc00::/7`, multicast, IPv4-mapped and NAT64 addresses are therefore all rejected, and an unparseable address fails closed. IP-literal URLs go through the same check.

**DNS pinning.** The default fetcher (`createPinnedHttpsFetcher`) connects to the address that was just validated, not to a fresh DNS answer, so DNS rebinding cannot move the connection to a private host. TLS still verifies the certificate for the original hostname (SNI is set to it), no `Accept-Encoding` compression is requested, and redirects are never followed by the fetcher itself. A custom `fetcher` receives the validated `address` and `family` and is responsible for honouring them.

It records content type, status, retrieval time, publisher hostname, excerpt and content hash. The caller must still treat captured content as untrusted. The implementation does not execute scripts or follow instructions embedded in source or provider content. No application route calls `captureSource()` yet; wiring it to user-supplied URLs needs authentication, per-principal rate limits and an operator allow list or egress policy in addition to the checks above.

`verifyEvidencePacket()` performs narrow deterministic checks for schema completeness, raw-artifact hash integrity, safe URL/capture metadata, excerpt matching, freshness when a window is supplied, claim coverage, duplicate and independent-source signals, unsupported claims, contradictions and supplied arithmetic. Results are typed (`complete`, `incomplete`, `needs_human_review`, or `check_failed`); they are not a truth score. A missing source snapshot is not proof that the source was safely captured: `safe_source_capture` and `excerpt_matches_snapshot` report `incomplete` for any source without a captured snapshot (a present but unsafe or mismatched snapshot is still `check_failed`), and `hash_integrity` is `incomplete` when no raw artifact was supplied to compare against.

## Evidence-quality policy layer

This layer lives in `src/evidence/evidence-quality.js`. It is an additional policy layer over the immutable evidence manifest; it does not silently upgrade the meaning of `workrelay.evidence-manifest.v1`. It adds: exact-host publisher authority classification constrained by registered subject scope and permitted claim types, claim-type freshness windows, source-snapshot verification, claim/source referential integrity, claim-level supporting/contradicting relationships, publisher-group corroboration candidates, and packet-bound human review attestations. The implementation and defaults are described in `docs/EVIDENCE_QUALITY_POLICY.md`.

Important boundaries:

- Authority tiers come only from an explicitly supplied, reviewed exact-host registry. WorkRelay does not infer authority from a domain name, page design, search ranking or provider assertion. No universal trusted-source registry is bundled.
- Freshness is evaluated against a supplied evaluation time and policy. Missing publication dates on material claims require human review; stale records are incomplete; implausibly future timestamps fail closed.
- Two domains or two content hashes are only corroboration candidates. Distinct domains do not prove independent reporting. An accepted human review must attest independence for the exact supporting source IDs and publisher groups before the corroboration check can be considered complete.
- Every material claim needs an explicit supporting source relationship. Dangling references and unsupported relationship types fail closed. Contradictory links require human adjudication.
- `recordEvidenceReview()` creates a canonical, SHA-256-committed `workrelay.evidence-review.v2` record bound to the exact evidence packet digest. In addition to claim and independence findings, v2 requires explicit in-scope/out-of-scope assessments for every material claim/source pair. The publisher authority registry is `workrelay.publisher-authority-registry.v2`; each exact-host entry carries subject scope and a permitted claim-type allowlist. The records are human declarations, not cryptographic signatures or identity proofs. Durable storage and authenticated reviewer accounts belong to the later operational-hardening phase. v1 review and registry records do not satisfy the v2 contracts and must be re-reviewed/re-issued; legacy exact-host maps remain unversioned and review-required.
- The demo evidence fixture intentionally remains bad: it uses an unavailable source and an unsupported claim. The new quality panel should show those defects rather than manufacture a clean score.

Neither verifier establishes factual truth, source authenticity, legal authority, actual publisher independence, authorship or provider quality. A human reviewer remains responsible for interpreting the source material, context, conflicts and limitations.
